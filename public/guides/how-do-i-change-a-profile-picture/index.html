How to Change Your Profile Picture (Any Platform)

Guide · Updated Aug 2026

To change your profile picture, go to your profile, tap or click your current photo, choose upload or take a new photo, then save — the exact menu wording differs slightly by platform (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Google, TikTok, and X each have their own path, detailed below). If your new photo doesn't appear right away, it's almost always a caching delay, not a failed upload.

How to Change Your Profile Picture on Any Platform

Every platform hides this in a slightly different menu. Here's the fastest path for each of the major ones, plus what to do if the new photo won't stick.

Facebook

On desktop or mobile web, click Profile picture, click Upload new photo, then follow the on-screen instructions to save your changes. In the mobile app, tap your profile picture in the top right of Facebook, tap your profile name, then tap the Camera in the bottom right of your profile picture, and select or upload a photo from there. Facebook also lets you make a picture temporary — click your profile picture, click Choose profile picture, select Upload Photo or Add Frame, click Make Temporary, and select when to switch back to your previous profile picture.

Instagram

In the app, open Instagram and log in, tap your profile picture, select Edit Profile, tap Change Profile Picture, then choose a new photo from your camera roll or take a new one. You can also do this from a desktop browser, though it doesn't offer the same cropping tools as the mobile app. Changes are near-instant — your new profile picture appears across Instagram within a few seconds, though it may take a minute or two to propagate to every surface.

LinkedIn

On desktop, visit your Profile, click your Profile Picture to open a dialog box, then click Add Photo. Click Use camera to take a new photo, or click Upload photo to add one already on your computer, then click Save. On mobile, tap your Profile Picture, tap View Profile, tap your Profile Picture again, tap View or edit profile photo, then Add Photo and Save. LinkedIn's current spec calls for 400 x 400 pixels minimum, 8 MB max file size, JPG or PNG only, displayed as a circle, so crop with that circular frame in mind.

Google / Gmail

Your Gmail photo is really your Google Account photo, and it updates everywhere at once. On desktop, open Gmail, click Profile at the top right, then Change profile picture. From the Google Account page directly, go to Personal info and select the photo option under Basic info. On mobile, open Gmail, tap Profile at the top right, then tap Change profile picture on your photo. It can take some time to update across all Google products, and by default your Gmail picture is visible to everyone — not just people within your organization or contacts you've messaged.

TikTok

On mobile, open TikTok, tap the Profile button in the bottom-right corner, tap Edit Profile, then tap Change Photo, and choose to shoot a new photo or pick one from your gallery. On desktop, hover over your profile picture in the top right, select My profile, tap Edit profile, and click your profile picture to swap it — you can only change images from the desktop version, not upload profile videos. TikTok's recommended image is at least 200x200 pixels in a 1:1 square aspect ratio, and since TikTok crops it into a circle, keep your face or logo centered.

X (Twitter)

On the web, click the profile icon, click Edit profile, click the camera icon in the profile picture section, select the picture you want to use, adjust the zoom, then click Apply and Save. On mobile, tap Profile, tap Edit Profile, then tap the camera icon on your current photo to upload or take a new one. X's recommended profile photo size is 400x400 pixels, and accepted file types are JPG, GIF, or PNG — X does not support animated GIFs for profile or header images.

Common Problems When Changing a Profile Picture

Photo won't upload or crop wrong

Most upload failures come down to three things: an outdated app, a shaky connection, or an image the platform's editor can't crop cleanly. Make sure you're using the latest app version or restart the app, check your connection, and refresh the page and try again. If the crop looks wrong, it's usually because the source photo isn't square — most platforms force a 1:1 circular or square crop, so a portrait or landscape photo will get cut unpredictably unless you pre-crop it to a square before uploading.

Old picture still showing (cache issue)

If the upload succeeded but contacts still see your old photo, it's almost always a caching delay rather than a failed save. Instagram notes a new picture may take a minute or two to propagate to every surface. Google gives a similar warning: it can take some time to update across all Google products associated with your account. If it's been more than a few hours, try logging out and back in, or clearing the app's cache — the photo is saved server-side, it just hasn't refreshed on every device yet.

How to Pick a Good Profile Picture Before You Upload It

None of the platform help pages cover this, but it matters more than the upload steps themselves — a technically perfect upload of a bad photo is still a bad profile picture.

Lighting and background basics

Even, front-facing light beats a dramatic angle every time — you want your face clearly visible, not partially shadowed. Natural light from a window works better than overhead ceiling lights, which cast shadows under the eyes and nose. Keep the background simple: a plain wall or blurred background keeps attention on your face, especially on LinkedIn, where choosing the right profile photo and presenting it professionally can significantly boost your online presence. Crop tight enough that your face fills a good portion of the frame — most platforms shrink profile photos into small circles, so a photo where you're a tiny speck in a wide shot loses all detail.

FAQ

How do I change my profile picture on my phone vs desktop?
The core steps are usually similar — find your profile, tap or click your current photo, upload a new one, save — but mobile apps typically include built-in cropping and filter tools that desktop browser versions lack. Instagram's desktop site, for instance, skips some of the editing options available in the app.

Why isn't my new profile picture showing up?
Almost always a propagation delay rather than a failed upload — give it a few minutes to a few hours, then try logging out and back in or clearing your app cache before assuming something went wrong.

What size or dimensions should a profile picture be?
It varies by platform: LinkedIn recommends 400 x 400 pixels minimum, 8 MB max file size, JPG or PNG only; X recommends 400x400 pixels; TikTok recommends at least 200x200 pixels in a 1:1 square aspect ratio. As a general rule, upload a square image at least 400x400 pixels and you'll clear most platforms' minimums comfortably.

Can I use an AI-generated photo as a profile picture?
Yes — there's no platform rule against it, and AI headshot tools have become a normal way to get a professional-looking photo without a studio session. Just be aware that any AI-generated image, including ones from our own tool, comes with the caveat that resemblance to your actual appearance isn't guaranteed, since the output is generated rather than photographed.
